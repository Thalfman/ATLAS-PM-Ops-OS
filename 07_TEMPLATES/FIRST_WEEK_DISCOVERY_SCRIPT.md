# FIRST_WEEK_DISCOVERY_SCRIPT.md

## Purpose

This script helps Tom ask useful PM/Ops, project-controls, reporting, and AI workflow questions during early onboarding, including periods when clearance or access limitations restrict direct work. It is designed to be respectful, non-disruptive, and security-conscious.

## Use posture

- Listen first.
- Do not ask for sensitive details outside approved settings.
- Do not request restricted documents or data before access is granted.
- Frame questions around process, pain points, interfaces, cadence, and expectations.
- Capture only what is appropriate for the tool/system being used.

## Opening positioning

```text
I’m coming in as a PM/Ops and project-controls resource with AI/ML fluency. My goal is to understand how the team already works, where reporting or coordination friction exists, and where I can improve project integrity without disrupting existing controls. I’ll stay inside approved tools and data boundaries, especially while clearance and access are still limited.
```

## Role and success criteria questions

1. What would make the first 30, 60, and 90 days successful from your perspective?
2. Which PM/Ops responsibilities need immediate support?
3. Where do reporting, schedule, finance, or action tracking issues most often create friction?
4. What should I avoid changing until I understand the environment better?
5. Which meetings or operating rhythms should I observe first?

## Reporting and communication questions

1. What reports are most important to leadership, customers, or internal stakeholders?
2. Which reports are hardest to produce or reconcile?
3. Where do narratives tend to need the most rework?
4. What makes a status update credible here?
5. Are there standard formats, review chains, or approval expectations I should learn first?

## Schedule and Microsoft Project questions

1. How is Microsoft Project used today: planning, baseline control, progress updates, dependency management, reporting, or all of the above?
2. Who owns schedule updates and who validates them?
3. What schedule health indicators are reviewed regularly?
4. Where do schedule variance narratives usually come from?
5. Are schedule exports or views controlled in a specific way?

## Finance, EVM, and project accounting questions

1. Which project controls or finance rhythms should I understand first?
2. How are cost, labor, schedule, and status reconciled?
3. What is the normal path for investigating discrepancies?
4. What makes a variance explanation acceptable here?
5. Who reviews finance/accounting narratives before they become official?

## Risk, issue, and discrepancy questions

1. How are risks, issues, and discrepancies tracked today?
2. What is the expected difference between a risk, an issue, and an action item?
3. Who owns triage and closure discipline?
4. Where do items tend to age or lose momentum?
5. Are there standard escalation thresholds?

## Knowledge capture and SOP questions

1. Where do process notes, SOPs, lessons learned, and working instructions live?
2. Which recurring process would benefit from clearer documentation?
3. Are there templates or naming conventions I should follow?
4. What lessons learned are most often repeated but not embedded into process?
5. Who approves SOP updates or process changes?

## AI workflow discovery questions

Use these only when appropriate and inside approved channels.

1. Which AI tools are approved for use today?
2. What categories of data are allowed in those tools?
3. Are there existing AI pilots or workflow-improvement efforts?
4. Where would AI-assisted drafting, summarization, reconciliation support, or action tracking be useful if done safely?
5. Who should review AI workflow ideas before any pilot is proposed?
6. Would synthetic demos be useful for discussing workflow possibilities without touching real data?

## Clearance-limited value questions

1. While access is limited, what safe work would be most useful for me to support?
2. Are there generic process maps, templates, or reporting structures I can help improve without restricted data?
3. Are there public or non-sensitive training materials I should study?
4. Can I observe unclassified/non-sensitive operating rhythms to understand interfaces and cadence?
5. Which administrative, documentation, or PM/Ops tasks are appropriate during this period?

## End-of-week reflection prompts

At the end of the first week, summarize:

- What operating rhythms exist?
- What reports matter most?
- Where does friction appear?
- Which workflows are already strong?
- Which improvements would be low-risk and high-value?
- Which AI opportunities require governance review before discussion?
- What should be learned next?
