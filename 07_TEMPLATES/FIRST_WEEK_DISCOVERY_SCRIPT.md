## Purpose

This script helps Tom ask useful PM/Ops, project-controls, reporting, and AI workflow discovery questions during early onboarding, including periods when clearance or access limitations restrict direct work. It is the general-purpose script; audience-split versions (manager, peers, IT, security, compliance, program leadership) live in `07_TEMPLATES/ONBOARDING_QUESTION_SET.md`. The script is designed to be respectful, non-disruptive, and security-conscious.

## Governance envelope

- **Data category** (per `06_GOVERNANCE/DATA_SENSITIVITY_DECISION_MODEL.md`, "Data categories"): Public-or-synthetic for the script itself; answers Tom captures are Tom-personal and stay in personal preparation notes unless generic enough for ATLAS.
- **Tool environment** (same file, "Tool environments"): ATLAS-local Markdown for the script; Tom-personal notes for tailored copies used in real conversations.
- **Review intensity** (per `06_GOVERNANCE/HUMAN_REVIEW_AND_AUDITABILITY_MODEL.md`, "Three review intensities"): Light. Tom self-reviews and tailors before each meeting.
- **Per-domain review pattern** (same file, "Per-domain review patterns"): Status and reporting outputs (when summarizing what was discussed).

## Use posture

- Listen first; ask second.
- Do not ask for sensitive details outside approved settings.
- Do not request restricted documents or data before access is granted.
- Frame questions around process, pain points, interfaces, cadence, and expectations.
- Capture only what is appropriate for the tool/system being used.
- Tailor each meeting: pick 3-5 questions from the relevant section; do not bring 20 questions when 4 would do.

## Opening positioning

```text
I’m coming in as a PM/Ops and project-controls resource with AI/ML fluency. My goal is to understand how the team already works, where reporting or coordination friction exists, and where I can improve project integrity without disrupting existing controls. I’ll stay inside approved tools and data boundaries, especially while clearance and access are still limited.
```

## First manager 1:1 questions

1. What would make the first 30, 60, and 90 days successful from your perspective?
2. Which PM/Ops responsibilities are highest priority for me to take on first?
3. What does "good" look like for a weekly status update or variance narrative in this environment?
4. What is your preferred cadence for our 1:1s, and how do you like status framed (verbal, written, dashboard)?
5. How do you prefer I escalate when I see a risk or issue I cannot resolve at my level?
6. What should I avoid changing in my first months until I understand the environment better?

Audience-split variations and follow-ups live in `07_TEMPLATES/ONBOARDING_QUESTION_SET.md` §A.

## Role and success criteria questions

1. What would make the first 30, 60, and 90 days successful from your perspective?
2. Which PM/Ops responsibilities need immediate support?
3. Where do reporting, schedule, finance, or action tracking issues most often create friction?
4. What should I avoid changing until I understand the environment better?
5. Which meetings or operating rhythms should I observe first?

## Stakeholder listening-tour questions

1. What would make my arrival the most useful for you in the next month?
2. Which interfaces between PM/Ops work and your work cause the most friction today?
3. What does a good handoff look like between your role and the PM role?
4. Are there documents I should read first to come up to speed responsibly?
5. Who else should I be talking to early?

Audience-split variations live in `07_TEMPLATES/ONBOARDING_QUESTION_SET.md` §B and §F.

## Reporting and communication questions

1. What reports are most important to leadership, customers, or internal stakeholders?
2. Which reports are hardest to produce or reconcile?
3. Where do narratives tend to need the most rework?
4. What makes a status update credible here?
5. Are there standard formats, review chains, or approval expectations I should learn first?

## Schedule and Microsoft Project questions

1. How is Microsoft Project used today: planning, baseline control, progress updates, dependency management, reporting, or all of the above?
2. Who owns schedule updates and who validates them?
3. What schedule health indicators are reviewed regularly?
4. Where do schedule variance narratives usually come from?
5. Are schedule exports or views controlled in a specific way?

## Finance, EVM, and project accounting questions

1. Which project controls or finance rhythms should I understand first?
2. How are cost, labor, schedule, and status reconciled?
3. What is the normal path for investigating discrepancies?
4. What makes a variance explanation acceptable here?
5. Who reviews finance/accounting narratives before they become official?

## Risk, issue, and discrepancy questions

1. How are risks, issues, and discrepancies tracked today?
2. What is the expected difference between a risk, an issue, and an action item?
3. Who owns triage and closure discipline?
4. Where do items tend to age or lose momentum?
5. Are there standard escalation thresholds?

## Knowledge management and Workspace questions

1. Where do process notes, SOPs, lessons learned, and working instructions live (Google Drive, internal wiki, document repository)?
2. Which Workspace features (Docs, Sheets, Drive search, NotebookLM-style features, "Help me write") are commonly used by PMs here, and which require approval?
3. Are there naming conventions, folder structures, or labeling expectations I should follow from day one?
4. How are templates and standard formats maintained, and who owns the master copy?
5. Which recurring process would benefit from clearer documentation?
6. What lessons learned are most often repeated but not embedded into process?
7. Who approves SOP updates or process changes?

## AI workflow discovery questions

Use only when the conversation is the right venue and rapport supports it. The full tool-approval question bank lives in `06_GOVERNANCE/EMPLOYER_TOOL_APPROVAL_QUESTION_SET.md`; this section is the short-form discovery subset.

1. Which AI tools are approved for use today, and for what categories of data?
2. Are there AI tools explicitly disallowed even for personal-style use on a work device?
3. Are there existing AI pilots or workflow-improvement efforts I should be aware of?
4. Where would AI-assisted drafting, summarization, reconciliation support, or action tracking be useful if done safely?
5. Who should review AI workflow ideas before any pilot is proposed?
6. Would synthetic demos be useful for discussing workflow possibilities without touching real data?
7. How should I handle the boundary between personal-preparation AI work and any future employer-deployable AI work?

Tom's posture for any AI conversation lives in `07_TEMPLATES/AI_INTEGRATION_DISCUSSION_GUIDE.md` and `06_GOVERNANCE/AI_CONVERSATION_GUIDE.md`.

## Clearance-limited contribution options

These questions help Tom and the manager agree on the week's commitments while clearance is pending.

1. While access is limited, what safe work would be most useful for me to support?
2. Are there generic process maps, templates, or reporting structures I can help improve without restricted data?
3. Are there public or non-sensitive training materials I should study?
4. Can I observe unclassified/non-sensitive operating rhythms to understand interfaces and cadence?
5. Are there synthetic demos of AI-assisted PM/Ops workflows you would like me to walk through with the team or with you, framed as personal preparation?
6. Which administrative, documentation, or PM/Ops tasks are appropriate during this period?
7. Are there observable artifacts you would find useful by Friday that I can deliver from public or synthetic inputs?

The standing menu Tom draws from lives in `07_TEMPLATES/CLEARANCE_LIMITED_VALUE_PLAN.md`; this week's selection becomes `07_TEMPLATES/WHAT_I_CAN_OFFER_THIS_WEEK.md`.

## Things not to do in week one

A short list. The point is to be a credible PM/Ops voice, not a fast one.

- Do not propose process or tool changes before observing how the work actually flows.
- Do not bring a personal AI tool to bear on anything that came from inside an employer system, even paraphrased.
- Do not ask for restricted documents, briefings, or data until access is in place.
- Do not name a specific AI product as a recommendation; learn the approved list first.
- Do not commit to deliverables that require cleared access in `07_TEMPLATES/WHAT_I_CAN_OFFER_THIS_WEEK.md`.
- Do not capture restricted content in personal notes, in ATLAS, in screenshots, or in filenames.
- Do not push back on a tool or process before understanding why it is the way it is.
- Do not skip the listening log in `07_TEMPLATES/LISTENING_PLAN.md`; week one is when the observations are freshest.
- Do not narrate AI capability or limitation in absolutes; use measured language.
- Do not appear to be a policy authority on AI - that role belongs elsewhere; Tom is a credible PM/Ops voice with a conservative posture.

## End-of-week reflection prompts

At the end of the first week, also consider:

- What operating rhythms exist?
- What reports matter most?
- Where does friction appear?
- Which workflows are already strong?
- Which improvements would be low-risk and high-value?
- Which AI opportunities require governance review before discussion?
