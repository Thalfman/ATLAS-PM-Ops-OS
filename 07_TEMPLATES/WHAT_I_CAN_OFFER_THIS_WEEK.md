## Purpose

A one-pager template Tom adapts weekly to communicate his PM/Ops and AI value during clearance-limited onboarding without overclaiming. It is the manager-shared expression of the menu in `07_TEMPLATES/CLEARANCE_LIMITED_VALUE_PLAN.md`. Per D-0044, it must not commit to scope outside what Tom and the manager have agreed.

## Governance envelope

- **Data category** (per `06_GOVERNANCE/DATA_SENSITIVITY_DECISION_MODEL.md`, "Data categories"): Tom-personal. The one-pager describes Tom's planned personal-preparation work; it does not contain employer data.
- **Tool environment** (same file, "Tool environments"): ATLAS-local Markdown for the template; the actual instance shared with the manager lives wherever the manager expects it (a Doc, a chat thread, a printed page). Do not store an employer-titled version in a personal AI tool.
- **Review intensity** (per `06_GOVERNANCE/HUMAN_REVIEW_AND_AUDITABILITY_MODEL.md`, "Three review intensities"): Standard. Tom self-reviews and signs off before sharing; manager is the accountable approver of the week's commitments.
- **Per-domain review pattern** (same file, "Per-domain review patterns"): Status and reporting outputs.

## Use posture

- Honest scope; nothing on the page that Tom cannot show evidence of by Friday.
- Visible artifacts only; no aspirational verbs ("explore," "consider," "look into") without a named output.
- Calibrated tone; neutral, accountable, not performative.
- Manager owns the answer on whether the week's commitments are appropriate. Tom's posture is "here is what I plan to do; tell me what to change."
- Re-issued weekly with a fresh date and a fresh commitment set. Do not edit last week's page in place.

## One-pager template

```text
What I can offer this week
Week of: [Synthetic placeholder: YYYY-MM-DD through YYYY-MM-DD]
Prepared by: [Tom's name and role]
Reviewer: [Manager's name and role]
Status: Draft for manager review

Context (one line)
I am operating in clearance-limited mode and using this week's time on personal-preparation work that is observable, conservative, and inside the ATLAS safety boundary.

Commitments this week (3-5)
- [Verb-led commitment 1] - Artifact: [path or named output] - By: [day]
- [Verb-led commitment 2] - Artifact: [path or named output] - By: [day]
- [Verb-led commitment 3] - Artifact: [path or named output] - By: [day]

What I am not committing to this week
- [Item 1, named at the scope level, not the topic level]
- [Item 2]
- [Item 3]

AI posture (one line)
AI in personal preparation only, on synthetic or public material, with my review on every output. No personal AI tool against employer data, ever.

What I am learning this week
- [Public study item with source: PMI, NIST, vendor public doc]
- [Synthetic practice item from ATLAS]

Question for you
[One specific question that helps you tell me what to change about scope, posture, or sequencing.]

Sign-off
- Drafted by Tom on [date].
- Reviewed by [manager] on [date].
- Adjustments after review: [list or "none"].
```

## Drafting checklist

Before sharing the one-pager with the manager, run this short check. Any "no" sends the page back for revision.

1. Every commitment has a verb, an artifact, and a date.
2. No commitment depends on cleared access Tom does not have.
3. No commitment requires real program, customer, contract, schedule, or finance content.
4. No commitment implies authority Tom has not been granted (e.g., "set up an AI workflow for the team").
5. The "not committing to" list is specific enough to be useful (e.g., not "anything classified" - more like "any analysis that requires real EVM data this period").
6. The AI posture line is honest about personal preparation; it does not promise employer-tool work that has not been approved.
7. The question for the manager is specific and answerable; not "any feedback?"
8. The total length fits one page when printed.

## Red flags (mirror W-15 §11 and §13)

Stop and revise if the draft contains any of these:

- A commitment Tom cannot produce evidence of by Friday.
- A commitment that would require Tom to paraphrase or restate restricted content in a personal note.
- A commitment that uses employer-titled placeholders (real program, real customer) instead of synthetic ones.
- A commitment that implies Tom owns a policy or scope outcome.
- Wording that overclaims expertise ("I will redesign the team's reporting workflow") rather than describing personal preparation ("I will draft a generic reporting checklist on a synthetic example").
- Any wording the manager might read as a workaround for missing access.

## When to escalate (rather than re-draft)

- Tom is asked for a commitment that would require restricted access - go back to the manager, restate the scope, do not silently expand.
- A "what I can offer" framing the manager rejected last week reappears in this week's draft - drop it and ask the manager what would be better.
- Manager's expectation has shifted (e.g., clearance moved to a later date) - re-baseline the menu before producing the next one-pager.

## Migration notes (post-clearance)

Once clearance lands and Tom is working against employer data inside approved tools, the same template can be adapted. The structure does not change; the data category, tool environment, and review intensity do. Migration triggers and re-approval steps are in `06_GOVERNANCE/AI_GOVERNANCE_NOTES.md` (D-0026) and `06_GOVERNANCE/AI_TOOL_APPROVAL_STRATEGY.md`. Carry forward to Phase 11 (Employer Migration Plan), not in this kit.
