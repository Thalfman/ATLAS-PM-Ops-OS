# EMPLOYER_TOOL_APPROVAL_QUESTION_SET.md

## Purpose

A question bank Tom can adapt for AI/tool governance conversations with managers, IT, security, compliance, or program leadership once it is appropriate to ask. The goal is to learn what is actually allowed, not to advocate for any specific tool, and not to imply that the employer must adopt anything.

This is personal preparation material. It does not state employer policy and does not assume a specific answer. Tom should review and tailor each question to the venue and audience before any real conversation.

## When to use these questions

- After onboarding has started and Tom has a sense of who the right person to ask is.
- In appropriate channels (manager 1:1, IT or security office hours, compliance intake, program kickoff).
- Not in the first week unless invited; not in unsolicited emails to busy stakeholders; not in front of customers.

If the answer to "is this a good moment to ask" is unclear, wait.

## Posture for every conversation

- Listen first; ask second.
- Do not claim knowledge of internal policy.
- Do not propose specific tools as a recommendation before learning what is allowed.
- Frame questions around safety, governance, and PM/Ops outcomes, not personal AI preferences.
- Treat every answer as authoritative for that context until contradicted by a higher authority.
- Capture answers in personal preparation notes only; never in employer systems unless the venue is the right place to record them.
- If an answer is "I do not know," accept it; do not push.

## Section A - Approved tools and approved data

1. Which AI tools are approved for employee use today, and for what categories of data?
2. Is there an internal page or document that lists the current approved AI tools?
3. Are there any AI tools that are explicitly disallowed, even for personal-style use on a work device?
4. How are new AI tools added to the approved list, and roughly how long does that take?
5. Are there approved AI capabilities (summarization, drafting, translation) where the tool itself is constrained, regardless of which product provides them?

## Section B - Data categories and AI use

6. Are there written rules for using AI tools with CUI, ITAR/export-controlled, proprietary, customer, contract, technical, financial, or schedule data?
7. Where is the line between "internal but unrestricted" and "internal and restricted" for AI input?
8. Are there data categories where AI use is explicitly forbidden, even with approved tools?
9. Are there categories where AI use is encouraged because the workflow is well understood?
10. How should program names, customer names, and contract identifiers be handled in AI inputs?

## Section C - Gemini, Workspace, and Microsoft tooling

11. Is Gemini Enterprise approved for internal document summarization, drafting, spreadsheet analysis, or workflow support? If yes, for which data categories?
12. Are there Workspace features (Docs, Sheets, Drive search, NotebookLM-style features, "Help me write") that have specific approval status?
13. Can AI be used with Microsoft Project outputs (exports, screenshots, narratives)? If yes, which fields or data classes are allowed?
14. Can AI be used with Excel content (formulas, narratives, data summaries)? If yes, what is the boundary?
15. Are there approved patterns for AI-assisted reporting in the internal reporting system?

## Section D - Logging, retention, and audit

16. Are there approved prompt logging, retention, or audit requirements for AI sessions?
17. Are AI outputs treated as records once they are used in official work? If so, where should they be stored?
18. Are there retention timelines specific to AI prompts and outputs, distinct from the underlying source data?
19. Should AI-assisted documents be labeled or watermarked in any way?
20. Are there reviewers or auditors who should be looped in for specific kinds of AI-assisted output?

## Section E - Meetings, notes, and communications

21. Are employees allowed to use AI for meeting note summarization? Under what conditions?
22. Are AI-based meeting recording or transcription tools approved? If so, which ones, and with what consent process?
23. Are there meeting types (customer, program, leadership, classified-adjacent) where AI tooling is not allowed even if the tool is otherwise approved?
24. Are there approved patterns for converting meeting notes into action items with AI assistance?
25. Are AI-drafted external communications (customer, partner, program office) subject to additional review?

## Section F - Workflow approval and piloting

26. Who approves new AI-enabled workflows, and what does the approval path look like?
27. What is the process for piloting a low-risk AI workflow on synthetic or sanitized data?
28. What documentation is required before migrating a local template into an employer workflow?
29. Are there ATLAS-style preparation patterns the team has seen work well (synthetic demos, standardized human review steps, etc.)?
30. Is there an existing intake form or template for proposing AI workflow improvements?

## Section G - Roles and responsibilities

31. Who owns AI policy questions in IT, security, and compliance?
32. Who in the program office is the right point of contact for AI questions that touch program data?
33. Are managers expected to approve their team's AI tool use, or is the approval handled centrally?
34. Who would be the accountable reviewer for an AI-assisted weekly status report, schedule narrative, or variance explanation?
35. Are there community-of-practice groups for AI integration that PM/Ops staff are encouraged to join?

## Section H - Personal-vs-employer separation

36. Are there guidelines for what should never cross between personal AI tools and employer tools, in either direction?
37. Is it acceptable to use personal AI tools for purely personal preparation (study, generic templates, public reading) on personal devices and accounts?
38. Are there expectations about what employees should not retain on personal devices after onboarding (briefing materials, screenshots, exports)?
39. Are there expectations about what should be wiped or transferred when a workflow migrates from personal preparation into an employer environment?
40. Are there boundaries between AI use for "learning the role" and AI use "for the role" that the team enforces?

## How to use this file in practice

Before a conversation:

1. Pick three to five questions from the relevant sections; do not bring the whole list.
2. Reorder them so the lowest-stakes question comes first.
3. Add context for the venue ("I want to be sure I'm preparing the right way for...").
4. Decide what you will do with each possible answer (act, wait, escalate, drop).

After a conversation:

1. Note the answers in personal preparation only; do not paraphrase restricted detail into personal tools.
2. Update Section I with any new open questions surfaced.
3. If an answer changes ATLAS posture or backlog, log a decision in `10_DECISION_LOG/DECISION_LOG.md`.

## What this file is not

- It is not a list of demands.
- It is not a policy document.
- It is not an audit script.
- It is not a sales pitch for any AI tool.

Treat it as a personal interview prep file. Use it to listen better, not to talk more.
